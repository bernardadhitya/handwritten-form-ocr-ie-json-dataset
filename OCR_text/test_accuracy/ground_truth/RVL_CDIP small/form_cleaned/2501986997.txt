TWELVE-MONTH INHALATION STUDY ON ROOM-AGED CIGARETTE SIDESTREAM SMOKE (RASS) IN RATS. H.-J. Haussmann, B. Gerstenberg, W. Göcke, P. Kuhl, G. Schepers, R. Stabbert, W. Stinn, A. Teredesai, F. Tewes, E. Anskeit, and P. Terpstra. INBIFO, Köln, Germany, and CRC, Zaventem, Belgium.  

Female Wistar rats were whole-body exposed to RASS, an environmental tobacco smoke surrogate, at 6 and 12 µg total particulate matter/l for 12 h/d, 5 d/week, 12 months. For comparison, another group of rats was head-only exposed at 12 µg/l for 7 h/d. Whole-body exposure affected the dose for key RASS constituents by (1) reducing the concentrations of nicotine, formaldehyde, and catechol and (2) by additional uptake by routes other than inhalation as shown by analysis of the urinary nicotine metabolites. No sign of pulmonary inflammation was seen (differential counting of bronchoalveolar lavage cells). There was a slight increase in oxidative DNA damage (8-hydroxy-deoxyguanosine) in the nasal respiratory epithelium but not in total pulmonary DNA. Histopathological changes were minimal to moderate reserve cell hyperplasia and squamous metaplasia as well as minimal olfactory epithelium atrophy; slight eosinophilic globules in sustentacular cells of the olfactory epithelium; pronounced squamous metaplasia and hyperplasia in the larynx at the base of epiglottis; and slight reserve cell hyperplasia in the bronchi. The hyper- and metaplastic changes are considered adaptive and were similar to those seen in subchronic sidestream smoke inhalation studies in rats.
