Coumarin.

Because of an erroneous declaration of coumarin being a carcinogen of category I, the use of coumarin is prohibited worldwide.

This classification is based on 1) the controversial results by Baer and Griepentrog, whose findings of bladder carcinoma with high doses could not be confirmed by a committee of international pathologists; rather, the malformation involved cholangioma and non-malignant proliferations; 2) the erroneous citation as a carcinogen in two species in the NIOSH register, mistakenly taken up from the Roe and Salaman experiment.

Beyond that, no metastasis has been observed. In contrast, 3 different studies established an anti-carcinogenic activity of coumarin. Coumarin exhibited no teratogenic activity and no mutagenic activity in the Ames test with 3 different strains.

If we disregard the different degradation mechanisms of coumarin in various animals in comparison with man, for example, the human organism converts 90% of coumarin to 7-hydroxy coumarin with complete excretion within 3 hrs and the difference in the main metabolites of o-hydroxyphenylacetic acid and 3-hydroxycoumarin, which are essentially responsible for the hepatotoxic activity, then there are no effective levels left for any histological and physiological activities, particularly for the enzymatic activity of the liver and toxic effects.

Studies of the effect of coumarin by smoke inhalation cannot produce any results, since the amount of coumarin transfer to smoke with permissible amounts of 1/2000 is on the no effect level and any possible activity cannot be detected.
