Topic - Ammunition of Flue-Cured Tobacco

Background - Flue-cured tobaccos grown during the 1976 and 1977 seasons are notable because they have unusually high levels of nicotine. These tobaccos may cause problems in their utilization because they produce cigarettes with a high amount of smoke nicotine. One approach toward modifying these tobaccos for improved utility would be to carry them through RDR's desiccation process. To study the effects of the desiccation process at least three distinct steps must be considered: ammunition, steaming, and the combination of ammunition plus steaming. The purpose of this study was to determine how each of these steps changes the composition of the flue-cured tobacco and smoke of cigarettes made from the processed tobacco.

Results  See RDR 1978 No. 9
1. Ammunition of flue-cured tobacco greatly affects leaf and smoke chemistry.
2. Desiccation of flue-cured tobacco reduces leaf and smoke nicotine and makes the smoke much milder.
3. DNCE is much like burley tobacco.
4. Ammunition or desiccation can be used to increase smoke pH.
5. Ammunition or desiccation of flue-cured tobacco may be used to prepare a uniquely flavored tobacco.
