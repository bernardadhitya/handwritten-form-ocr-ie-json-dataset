CHLORIDES IN TOBACCO - Aqueous tobacco extracts were fairly well clarified by the use of copper sulfate and calcium hydroxide, and their presence did not influence results or brown precipitation of potassium chloride. Good results were obtained on samples of chewing tobacco when the same tobacco was extracted by standing in contact with water, the tobacco particles removed by filtration, the filtrate clarified with copper sulfate and calcium hydroxide, and the chloride content determined by the Volhard method. The results averaged about 6.2% high as those obtained by the regular gravimetric method. When the purified extracts were made slightly acid with acetic acid and the chloride content determined by the smoke method, the results averaged about 6.2% high as those obtained by the Volhard method.

Various ammonium salts and calcium hydroxide were tried as clarifying agents, but this combination appeared not to be quite as good as that employing copper sulfate. Hydrogen peroxide, proving not to interfere with the determination of chloride by the Volhard method, was also investigated for its value in removing the color from aqueous tobacco extracts. It was mixed with the tobacco-water mixture and the filtered extract on which chloride was then determined by the Volhard method. The results on the filtered extract were fairly good, but the end point color in the titration was not as distinct as those of the previously described procedure.

In simplifying the method using copper sulfate and calcium hydroxide, the tobacco-water mixture was decanted, clarified and filtered after contact with salts, and the mixture treated with potassium cyanide until the filter just showed a faint yellow color.
